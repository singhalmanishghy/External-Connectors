How custom software is put together

Three layers. Your developers build the first, you rent the third, and the middle one is where an architect makes most decisions.

Custom software = Core app + Integration layer + External capabilities

  1. A customer pays through a payment link. The money moves at the gateway, outside your app.
  2. The gateway calls your webhook inbox with the news: paid.
  3. The inbox checks the signature (proof the message really came from the gateway) and puts the event in a queue.
  4. The backend takes the event from the queue and marks the invoice paid in the database.
  5. The backend asks the WhatsApp adapter to send a receipt. The adapter calls WhatsApp’s API using the stored key.

The architect’s job. Choose each vendor, decide what happens when one fails, keep the keys safe, and know what every call costs. Swap “payment” for “IndiaMART lead” or “call recording” and the same pattern repeats.

    Architect’s primer

    Fifteen ideas that come up in every integration. Learn these and vendor documentation starts to make sense.

    How apps talk

    API
    Your app asks another service to do something, like a waiter carrying an order to the kitchen. Example: “send this WhatsApp”.
    Webhook
    The other service tells your app when something happens, like the kitchen ringing a bell. Example: “payment received”.
    SDK
    A ready-made kit from the vendor that runs inside your app or phone app. Examples: GPS tracking, Truecaller login.
    OAuth (“Connect your Google account”)
    The user lets your app act on their account without sharing a password, like a valet key that opens only some doors and can be taken back.
    No API
    Some systems offer no API. You then use a small connector on the client’s computer (Tally, weighbridges) or browser automation (government portals).

    Making it reliable

    Rate limits
    Vendors cap how many calls you can make per second or per day. Send in batches and slow down when told, so a busy day doesn’t block the app.
    Retries and idempotency
    Networks fail, so calls get retried. An idempotency key makes sure a retried payment or message still happens only once.
    Queues and background jobs
    Slow work, such as sending 500 WhatsApps or reading 50 PDFs, goes into a queue so users never wait on a spinner.
    Sandbox and live
    Most vendors offer a test mode with fake money and test numbers. Build and demo there, then switch keys to go live.
    Logs and monitoring
    Record every outside call and its result. When a client says “the message never came”, the log answers in seconds.

    Business and risk

    Keys and secrets
    API keys are passwords for apps. Keep them on the server or in a vault, never inside website or phone-app code.
    Adapters
    Wrap each vendor behind your own small adapter. Switching from MSG91 to Gupshup then means changing one piece, not the whole app.
    Unit costs
    Most services charge per use: per message, minute, page or transaction. Put these into your quote, either passed through or bundled with a margin.
    Data protection and location
    Know which personal data goes to each vendor and where it is stored. India’s DPDP Act and some clients require care, sometimes Indian data centres.
    Multi-tenancy
    One app serving many client companies, each seeing only its own data. Essential when a custom build becomes a product.

    Built into the app itself

    Developers code these directly, at most with a free library. No outside service and no monthly bill.

    Security basics

    • Creating and checking OTP codes (only the delivery is outside)
    • Passwords, sessions, roles and permissions
    • Audit trail of who changed what, and when
    • Format checks for GSTIN, PAN, IFSC and pincode (checking they really exist is outside)

    Business logic

    • GST, discount and total calculations; amounts in words in lakh and crore
    • PDF quotes and invoices using a library
    • QR code and barcode images; charts and dashboards
    • Excel and CSV import and export
    • Reminders and scheduled jobs (the hosting supplies the timer)
    • Duplicate-lead checks and lead assignment rules

    Phone and browser features

    • Camera capture, file upload and GPS reading while the app is open
    • Offline use and install-to-home-screen (PWA)
    • Fingerprint or face login (passkeys)
    • Tap-to-call and tap-to-WhatsApp links

    Before you pick a vendor

    Ask each one these questions. The answers decide cost, speed and lock-in.

    • Is there an API with public documentation, and does it send webhooks?
    • Is there a free test mode (sandbox) for development?
    • What does each unit cost (message, call, user, transaction), and who pays: you or the client?
    • Where is the data stored, and can you export all of it if you leave?
    • Which approvals or KYC are needed, and how long do they take?

    Account ownership. Open KYC-bound accounts in the client’s name: payment gateway, WhatsApp Business, SMS DLT and GST access. Your team gets user access. Moving these accounts later means redoing KYC.
